## 📚 Modules Included in `index.html` (55 High-Yield SBA Cards)

### 1. Malaria in Emergency Medicine (Cards 1–5)
- **Severe Falciparum Criteria**: Parasitaemia > 2% + metabolic acidosis (lactate > 4.0 mmol/L, HCO3 < 15 mmol/L), GCS < 11.
- **Emergency Resuscitation & Therapeutics**: IV Artesunate (2.4 mg/kg at 0, 12, 24h) superiority over IV Quinine; cardiac & glucose monitoring.
- **Non-Falciparum Radical Cure**: *P. vivax* / *P. ovale* liver hypnozoite clearance with Primaquine; mandatory pre-treatment **G6PD activity documentation**.
- **Emergency Diagnostic Pathways**: Serial thick & thin blood films at 12–24h and 48h to rule out malaria.
- **Special Populations & PADH**: Post-Artesunate Delayed Haemolysis recognition and weekly Hb monitoring.
- *Guidelines*: British Infection Association (BIA), RCEMLearning, UKHSA, and NICE CKS.

### 2. Acute Breast Conditions (Cards 6–10)
- **Physiological Milk Engorgement**: Bilateral fullness days 2–6 postpartum, supportive care, avoiding unnecessary antibiotics.
- **Infective Lactational Mastitis**: Oral Flucloxacillin 500 mg QDS for 10–14 days and mandatory continuation of breastfeeding.
- **Breast Abscess Intervention**: Ultrasound scan followed by **ultrasound-guided needle aspiration** first-line over open incision & drainage (I&D).
- **Non-Lactational Periductal Mastitis (Zuska's Disease)**: Smoking-related, anaerobic flora, oral Co-amoxiclav, fistula prevention.
- **Red Flag Mimic — Inflammatory Breast Cancer (IBC)**: *Peau d'orange*, antibiotic failure, urgent 2-Week Wait (2WW) referral.
- *Guidelines*: NICE CKS, NICE NG12, and RCEMLearning.

### 3. STI-Related Vaginal Discharge (Non-Pregnant) (Cards 11–15)
- **Pelvic Inflammatory Disease (PID)**: Outpatient triple therapy (Ceftriaxone 1 g IM + Doxycycline 100 mg BD 14d + Metronidazole 400 mg BD 14d) and retaining an in-situ IUD for 48–72h.
- **Neisseria gonorrhoeae**: Ceftriaxone 1 g IM monotherapy, stewardship withdrawal of azithromycin, and mandatory Test of Cure at 14 days.
- **Chlamydia trachomatis**: First-line Doxycycline 100 mg BD for 7 days (superiority over single-dose azithromycin for rectal co-infection).
- **Trichomonas vaginalis vs. Bacterial Vaginosis**: Colpitis macularis ("strawberry cervix"), elevated pH > 4.5, Metronidazole 400 mg BD for 7 days, mandatory partner tracing.
- **Fitz-Hugh-Curtis Syndrome (Perihepatitis)**: RUQ pleuritic pain mimic, Glisson's capsule "violin-string" adhesions, inpatient IV triple therapy.
- *Guidelines*: BASHH National Guidelines, RCOG Green-top Guideline No. 32, and NICE CKS.

### 4. Emergency Regional Anaesthesia & Nerve Blocks (Cards 16–20)
- **Fascia Iliaca Compartment Block (FICB)**: Ultrasound-guided "bow-tie" sign (sartorius over iliopsoas), in-plane lateral-to-medial hydrodissection under fascia iliaca with 30–40 mL dilute LA for #NOF; opioid-sparing delirium reduction. *(Includes vector ultrasound sonoanatomy guide).*
- **Serratus Anterior Plane Block (SAPB)**: 4th–5th rib mid-axillary coronal view, needle placement superficial or deep to serratus anterior for multiple rib fractures (T2–T9 hemithorax analgesia) avoiding pleura. *(Includes vector ultrasound sonoanatomy guide).*
- **Forearm Peripheral Nerve Blocks**: Median nerve identification between FDS and FDP ("honeycomb" fascicles), ulnar and radial nerve landmarks. *(Includes cross-sectional sonoanatomy guide).*
- **Bier's Block (IVRA)**: Mandatory 0.5% plain Prilocaine (3 mg/kg), double-cuff tourniquet, strict 20-minute minimum inflation rule, methaemoglobinaemia reversal with methylene blue. *(Includes procedural safety diagram).*
- **Local Anaesthetic Systemic Toxicity (LAST)**: AAGBI protocol: 20% Lipid Emulsion (Intralipid 1.5 mL/kg bolus over 1 min, then 15 mL/kg/hr infusion); ACLS modifications (adrenaline < 1 mcg/kg, zero vasopressin/lidocaine). *(Includes complete AAGBI resuscitation flowchart).*
- *Guidelines*: RCEM Best Practice Guidelines, NICE CG124, Association of Anaesthetists (AAGBI) Safety Guidelines.

### 5. Paediatric Fractures & Orthopaedic Emergencies (Cards 21–25)
- **Supracondylar Humeral Fractures (Card 21)**: **Authentic Clinical Radiograph** illustrating extension displacement; Anterior Humeral Line (AHL) missing the capitellum; joint effusion with anterior "Sail Sign" and pathological posterior fat pad; AIN nerve motor testing (FPL/FDP pincer "OK" sign; zero sensory loss); BOAST 20°–30° backslab and urgent theatre reduction.
- **Salter-Harris Physeal Fractures (Card 22)**: **Authentic Clinical Radiograph** demonstrating physeal disruption with articular involvement; SALTR classification (Types I–V); Thurston-Holland fragment; why children cannot remodel intra-articular steps (>2 mm step mandates ORIF) to prevent transphyseal bony bridge growth arrest.
- **Distal Radius Torus (Buckle) Fractures (Card 23)**: **Authentic Clinical Radiograph** displaying cortical buckling on compression side with intact volar cortex; **UK FORCE Trial (Lancet 2022)** & **NICE NG38** protocol: removable splint / bandage for comfort, direct ED discharge, and zero routine fracture clinic follow-up or repeat X-rays.
- **Forearm Fracture-Dislocations & Radiocapitellar Alignment (Card 24)**: **Authentic Clinical Radiograph** of Monteggia fracture-dislocation showing ulnar shaft fracture with dislocated radial head; radiocapitellar line missing the capitellum; "MUGR" rule (Monteggia = Ulnar, Galeazzi = Radial); mandatory elbow and wrist views.
- **Non-Accidental Injury (NAI) vs Accidental Toddler's Fracture (Card 25)**: **Authentic Clinical Radiograph** of paediatric tibial fracture; distinguishing accidental toddler's spiral hairline fracture from high-specificity physical abuse fractures (Classic Metaphyseal Lesions / corner and bucket-handle fractures, posterior rib fractures, infant femur fractures); UK Safeguarding protocol (NICE CG89 & RCPCH).
- *Guidelines*: British Orthopaedic Association Standards for Trauma (BOAST), NICE NG38, NICE CG89, RCPCH Child Protection Companion, and RCEMLearning.

### 6. UK Mental Health Law in the Emergency Department (Cards 26–30)
- **Holding Powers & Section 5(2) Prohibition (Card 26)**: Why **Section 5(2) and Section 5(4) CANNOT be used in the ED** (statutory restriction to admitted hospital inpatients); applying the **Mental Capacity Act 2005 (Section 4B & Best Interests)** and **Common Law (Doctrine of Necessity)** to restrain and administer life-saving treatment (e.g. NAC in paracetamol toxicity).
- **Section 136 MHA & Policing and Crime Act 2017 (Card 27)**: Statutory reduction of detention to **24 hours** (extendable once by up to 12h only by a doctor for medical delay); **absolute ban on police cells for under-18s**; role of the ED as a place of safety for acute physical needs; police custody handover rules.
- **Mental Capacity Act vs Mental Health Act Interface (Card 28)**: The critical boundary: **The Mental Health Act treats MENTAL disorders ONLY (Section 63)** and CANNOT be used to compel treatment for unrelated physical surgical emergencies (e.g. perforated appendicitis); autonomy of a capacitous patient to refuse life-saving treatment; when the MCA applies.
- **Emergency Admission Sections: S2 vs S3 vs S4 (Card 29)**: Section 2 (Assessment, up to 28 days, non-renewable, 2 doctors + AMHP, ideal for first presentations); Section 3 (Treatment, up to 6 months, renewable, treatment must be available, Nearest Relative can object); Section 4 (Emergency, 72 hours, 1 doctor, rare in ED).
- **De-escalation, Physical Restraint & Rapid Tranquillisation (Card 30)**: **NICE NG10** protocol: de-escalation first; never maintain prone restraint (positional asphyxia risk); parenteral regimens: **IM Lorazepam (1–2 mg)** or **IM Haloperidol (2–5 mg) + IM Promethazine (25–50 mg)**; avoiding haloperidol in stimulant toxicity/prolonged QTc; **mandatory vital signs monitoring every 15 minutes for the 1st hour, then every 30 minutes**.
- *Guidelines*: Mental Health Act 1983 (amended 2007), Mental Capacity Act 2005, Policing and Crime Act 2017, NICE NG10, and RCEM Mental Health in the ED Toolkit.

### 7. ED Leadership, Staff Well-Being & Human Factors (SLO8) (Cards 31–35)
- **Staff Well-Being & Fatigue Management (Card 31)**: RCEM Curriculum 2021 **SLO8 ("Lead the ED Shift")** explicitly names **"looks after staff well-being – management of breaks and fatigue"** as a **mandatory, non-optional capability**; why crowding never justifies canceling breaks; cognitive slowing, drug calculation errors, and physiological risks of acute fatigue.
- **Graded Assertiveness in Challenging a Senior — PACE Framework (Card 32)**: Structured escalation in crisis resource management: **Probe** (inquiry: *"Are you aware that..."*), **Alert** (hazard: *"I am concerned that this carries a risk of..."*), **Challenge [C]** (firm halt: *"Please pause and stop what you are doing while we check..."*), and **Emergency** (*"STOP! Emergency halt!"*); avoiding passive abdication or aggressive hostility.
- **Conflict Management & Inter-Specialty Escalation (Card 33)**: Core SLO8 descriptors: *"Manage conflict"*, *"Appropriate assertiveness while maintaining professionalism"*, and *"Remain calm and professional at all times"*; managing surgical pushback in acute sepsis/peritonitis; eliminating circular arguments; timely **senior-to-senior (Consultant-to-Consultant) escalation** pathways.
- **Crisis Leadership, Role Modelling & Situational Awareness (Card 34)**: Managing extreme crowding (OPEL 4); emotional contagion and why panic/cynicism from shift leaders harms teams; **"Provide an acceptable role model"**; rapid 2-minute multidisciplinary crowding huddles; *Civility Saves Lives* evidence base (incivility causes a 61% decline in cognitive ability and 50% drop in diagnostic accuracy).
- **Post-Incident Debriefing & Second Victim Support (Card 35)**: Managing traumatic resuscitations (paediatric cardiac arrest); immediate **Hot Debrief (STOP / TALK model)** within minutes for factual clarity, validation, and staff well-being; delayed **Cold Debrief (1–2 weeks later)** for systems learning; **contraindication to mandatory single-session psychological debriefing (CISD)** under NICE NG116 and Cochrane evidence; supporting the "second victim" without blame.
- *Guidelines*: RCEM Emergency Medicine Curriculum 2021 (SLO8), RCEM Sustainable Working Practices, Clinical Human Factors Group (CHFG), Civility Saves Lives, NICE NG116, and GMC Good Medical Practice.

### 8. Tetanus Prophylaxis, Wound Care & Clinical Tetanus (Cards 36–40)
- **Tetanus Prophylaxis in Fully Vaccinated Patients (Card 36)**: UK Green Book Chapter 30 (Table 30.1) decision matrix; defining tetanus-prone wounds; why immunocompetent patients who completed the 5-dose UK schedule **NEVER receive Tetanus Immunoglobulin (TIG)**; indications for a single reinforcing booster dose (Td/IPV, Revaxis) if last dose was > 10 years ago.
- **Unvaccinated / Incomplete Vaccination with Tetanus-Prone Wounds (Card 37)**: Simultaneous passive (TIG) and active (Td/IPV) immunisation; standard TIG dose (250 IU IM) vs **doubled dose (500 IU IM)** triggers (presentation > 24 hours post-injury, body weight > 90 kg, or heavy soil/manure contamination); mandatory **separate anatomical site injection rule** using different syringes to prevent in-vivo antigen-antibody neutralisation.
- **Tetanus Prophylaxis in Immunosuppressed / Impaired Immunity Patients (Card 38)**: The statutory exception: patients receiving chemotherapy, immunosuppressive biologics, high-dose steroids, or with haematological malignancies/asplenia **must receive TIG for ANY tetanus-prone wound regardless of their past vaccination history or recent booster timing**; reasons for impaired anamnestic humoral response.
- **Pathophysiology of Clinical Tetanus & Early Presentation (Card 39)**: *Clostridium tetani* toxinology; **tetanospasmin** zinc-endopeptidase mechanism (retrograde intra-axonal transport to spinal cord Renshaw cells, cleavage of synaptobrevin/VAMP, selective blockade of inhibitory neurotransmitters **GABA and glycine** causing uninhibited alpha-motor neuron firing); clinical hallmarks: **trismus (lockjaw)** as earliest symptom (>80%), *risus sardonicus*, and opisthotonos.
- **Emergency Resuscitation & Therapeutics for Established Tetanus (Card 40)**: 5-pillar emergency resuscitation protocol: sensory isolation (dark, quiet room); early endotracheal intubation/tracheostomy before fatal laryngospasm; **Human Tetanus Immunoglobulin (HTIG 3,000–6,000 IU IM)** to neutralise unbound circulating toxin; **IV Metronidazole (500 mg TDS) first-line** and **strict contraindication to Benzylpenicillin** (competitive GABA antagonist that worsens spasms); spasm and autonomic storm control with **IV Benzodiazepines** and **IV Magnesium sulphate infusion**; mandatory **active 3-dose vaccination course post-recovery** (clinical tetanus does NOT confer immunity).
- *Guidelines*: UK Health Security Agency (UKHSA) Green Book Chapter 30 (Tetanus), RCEM Best Practice Guidelines on Tetanus Prophylaxis, Intensive Care Society (ICS), and NICE CKS.

### 9. Medical Statistics & Evidence-Based Testing (Cards 41–45)
- **Diagnostic Test Accuracy & 2×2 Contingency Tables (Card 41)**: Mathematical formulas for Sensitivity (TP / [TP + FN]), Specificity (TN / [TN + FP]), Positive Predictive Value (TP / [TP + FP]), and Negative Predictive Value (TN / [TN + FN]); why Sensitivity & Specificity are fixed intrinsic test properties while **PPV and NPV strictly depend on disease prevalence**; clinical consequences of screening low-prevalence populations.
- **Likelihood Ratios, Fagan Nomogram & ROC Curves (Card 42)**: Calculating +LR = Sens / (1 - Spec) and -LR = (1 - Sens) / Spec; interpretation thresholds (+LR > 10 and -LR < 0.1 for decisive shifts); Bayes' theorem pre-to-post-test probability conversions (e.g. Wells score PE rule-out with negative D-dimer); **Receiver Operating Characteristic (ROC)** curve interpretation (y = Sens, x = 1 - Spec; AUC of 1.0 vs 0.5).
- **Risk & Effect Measures: ARR, RRR, NNT & NNH (Card 43)**: Calculating Absolute Risk Reduction (ARR = CER - EER), Relative Risk Reduction (RRR = [CER - EER] / CER), and Number Needed to Treat (NNT = 1 / ARR); Number Needed to Harm (NNH = 1 / ARI); mandatory **rounding rules** (NNT always rounded UP, NNH always rounded DOWN); the **rare disease assumption** when Odds Ratio (OR) approximates Relative Risk (RR) (< 10%).
- **Hypothesis Testing, Errors, Statistical Power & Confidence Intervals (Card 44)**: Null hypothesis (H0) vs Alternative hypothesis (H1); **Type I error (alpha, false positive)** vs **Type II error (beta, false negative)**; Statistical Power (1 - beta, >= 80%) and its 4 determinants; why p > 0.05 in an underpowered trial represents high risk of Type II error (*"Absence of evidence is not evidence of absence"*); **Null Value Rules** for 95% Confidence Intervals (0 for difference measures; 1.0 for ratio measures).
- **Statistical Test Selection Algorithm (Card 45)**: The 3-step decision tree: Continuous Normal (Parametric) vs Continuous Skewed / Ordinal (Non-Parametric) vs Categorical; Independent vs Paired; 2 groups vs >= 3 groups; Student's t-test vs Mann-Whitney U; Paired t-test vs Wilcoxon signed-rank; ANOVA vs Kruskal-Wallis; **Chi-squared vs Fisher's exact test** (mandatory when expected cell counts < 5).

### 10. Study Design, Critical Appraisal & Meta-Analysis (Cards 46–50)
- **Epidemiological Study Designs & Hierarchy of Evidence (Card 46)**: Prospective Cohort studies (starts with exposure, measures incidence and **Relative Risk**; vulnerable to attrition bias) vs Case-Control studies (starts with disease/outcome, looks backward for exposure, measures **Odds Ratio**; vulnerable to **Recall Bias**) vs Cross-Sectional studies (measures **Prevalence**; cannot establish temporal causality); Ecological study & ecological fallacy.
- **Clinical Trial Methodology: Randomization, Concealment, Blinding & ITT (Card 47)**: **Allocation Concealment** (operates *before* randomisation to prevent **Selection Bias**, e.g. central automated web allocation) vs **Blinding** (operates *after* randomisation to prevent Performance & Detection bias); **Intention-to-Treat (ITT)** analysis (*"Once randomised, always analysed"*; preserves baseline prognostic balance, reflects real-world effectiveness, prevents attrition bias) vs Per-Protocol analysis.
- **Systematic Reviews & Forest Plot Interpretation (Card 48)**: Anatomy of a meta-analysis Forest Plot: point estimate square box, **box area/size = statistical weighting** (inverse variance), horizontal whiskers = 95% CI; **the summary diamond** (apex = pooled effect, width = pooled 95% CI); **vertical line of no effect** (1.0 for ratios, 0 for differences); interpreting statistically significant protective effects.
- **Heterogeneity & Publication Bias in Meta-Analysis (Card 49)**: Cochrane thresholds for the **I-squared statistic** (0-40% low, 30-60% moderate, 50-90% **substantial**, 75-100% considerable); when to use a **Fixed-Effect** vs **Random-Effects model** (mandatory when I-squared > 50%); **Funnel Plot asymmetry** and Egger's regression test to detect **Publication Bias** (the "file drawer" phenomenon where small negative studies are unpublished).
- **Epidemiological Biases & Confounding in Clinical Research (Card 50)**: Defining a true **confounder** (associated with exposure, independent cause of outcome, *not* an intermediate step); methods to control confounding in design (randomization, matching) vs analysis (multivariable logistic regression, stratification); distinguishing **Lead-Time Bias** (illusion of survival due to earlier detection on the clock) from **Length-Time Bias** (disproportionate detection of indolent, slow-growing tumours).
- *Guidelines*: Oxford Centre for Evidence-Based Medicine (CEBM), Cochrane Handbook for Systematic Reviews of Interventions, CONSORT, STROBE, and BMJ Statistics Notes.

### 11. Paediatric Safeguarding & Non-Accidental Injury (NAI) (Cards 51–55)
- **Patterns of Bruising & Clinical Prediction Rules (Card 51)**: The core tenet: *"Those who don't cruise rarely bruise"* (< 1% of non-mobile infants have accidental bruises); the **TEN-4-FACES** clinical prediction rule (Torso, Ears, Neck in < 4 years, ANY bruising in < 4 months, Frenulum tear, Angle of jaw, Cheek, Eyelids, Subconjunctival); soft tissue vs bony prominence topography; mandatory **Section 47 referral**, paediatric admission, and coagulation screen (excluding Haemophilia/vWD).
- **Fracture Specificity & Skeletal Survey Protocols (Card 52)**: High-specificity physical abuse fractures: **Classic Metaphyseal Lesions (CMLs)** (corner and bucket-handle microfractures through primary spongiosa from traction/torsion shaking), **Posterior Rib Fractures** (from manual thoracic squeezing levering rib heads over transverse processes; *never caused by infant CPR*), scapular/spinous/sternal fractures; **BSPR Skeletal Survey Protocol** in children < 2 years and **mandatory repeat limited survey at 11–14 days** to detect healing callus; excluding Osteogenesis Imperfecta (COL1A1/COL1A2, wormian bones) and Rickets.
- **Abusive Head Trauma (AHT / Shaken Baby Syndrome) & Neuroimaging (Card 53)**: Biomechanics of angular acceleration/deceleration tearing bridging cortical veins; the classic AHT triad: (1) **Subdural Haematoma** (interhemispheric, over convexities, or multi-compartment of differing ages); (2) **Encephalopathy** (diffuse axonal shear injury, hypoxic-ischaemic swelling); (3) **Multi-layered Retinal Haemorrhages** extending to the ora serrata with retinoschisis; why low-height domestic falls (< 3 feet) cannot generate these forces; mandatory **non-contrast CT brain**, dilated fundoscopy within 24–48h by an ophthalmologist, and follow-up **MRI Brain and Whole Spine** (for spinal subdural blood and ligamentous injury).
- **Statutory Framework, Consent & Emergency Legal Powers (Card 54)**: Children Act 1989 emergency holding powers: **Section 46 (Police Protection)** by police constable (detaining child in hospital for up to **72 hours** without a court order when at risk of significant harm); **Section 44 (Emergency Protection Order - EPO)** by Family Court (up to 8 days, extendable by 7 days); **Section 47** duty to investigate significant harm vs **Section 17** child in need; managing hostile parental refusal of consent (common law emergency treatment vs obtaining court orders for non-emergency skeletal surveys); Caldicott principles and duty to share information overriding parental confidentiality.
- **Non-Accidental Burns, Scalds & Fabricated or Induced Illness (Card 55)**: Distinguishing forced immersion scalds from accidental splash scalds: **symmetrical "glove" or "stocking" distribution**, **sharp horizontal tide-mark (waterline)**, **absence of splash marks** (held immobile), **flexural crease sparing** (popliteal/ankle creases spared from reflexive flexion), and **"doughnut sparing"** of central buttocks against cold bath porcelain; patterned contact burns (irons, circular 8–10 mm cigarette burns); **Fabricated or Induced Illness (FII / Perplexing Presentations - RCPCH 2021)**: doctor-shopping, symptom fabrication, induction of illness, and multi-agency strategy meetings.
- *Guidelines*: NICE Clinical Guideline CG89 (Child Maltreatment), RCPCH Child Protection Companion, British Society of Paediatric Radiology (BSPR), British Burn Association (BBA), and Children Act 1989.

## 🛠 Features

- **Interactive SBA Choices**: Click directly on options **A through E** for instant visual validation (green for correct, red for incorrect with retry prompt) and automatic rationale reveal.
- **Embedded Clinical Visuals & Statutory Algorithms**: Real plain radiographs for orthopaedic trauma and high-contrast statutory decision algorithms for mental health law.
- **Uncompressed Rationales**: Every card includes full physiological mechanisms, clinical thresholds, diagnostic cutoffs, case law, and trial evidence.
- **FRCEM SBA Trap Boxes**: Highlighted common examiner traps, distractors, and pitfalls.
- **Active Recall Prompts**: Dedicated clinical questions on every card.
- **Browser-Side Progress Tracking**: Scores, attempts, card mastery (*Mastered*, *Reviewing*, *Needs Work*), and personal revision notes persist locally via `localStorage`.
- **Zero External Dependencies**: Standalone HTML5/CSS/JavaScript with 100% self-contained embedded images—works completely offline.
